# Retention

## History retention

Automatic history expiry is opt-in in **Local storage → History retention**, initially showing 90 days. The period accepts 1–3,650 days and persists with the profile and its replica export. Saving an enabled policy permits permanent synchronized deletion of eligible old visits. Disabling expiry or increasing the period cannot restore already expired visits.

Only the source installation expires its own visits. Age uses the original `visited_at`, with a strict cutoff: visits at or newer than `now − days` remain. Relay receipt time never determines expiry. A peer's shorter policy cannot delete another source's history. The owner's wall clock determines expiry time; a clock jump can change eligibility, while merge ordering still uses causal clocks.

Unacknowledged visits stay protected, including unencrypted drafts, immutable encrypted uploads and known duplicate copies of the same native identity. An old queued visit uploads first; expiry can select it on a subsequent reconciliation after acknowledgement. Other pending operations, bookmark tombstones, sessions and active restoration work are not aged out by this feature.

A pass scans at most 500 indexed candidates and selects at most 100 visits. It writes ordinary selected-deletion proofs in protocol-sized groups of at most 80 identities. Deletion, local projection/content cleanup, counter reservation and persistent scan progress commit together. Failed writes roll back the pass. The scan advances past protected visits, wraps after exhaustion and revisits them after their uploads commit. An empty range does not scan the history journal.

Passes run after the initial relay pull/check during synchronization, before the existing authenticated history purger. The owner must reconnect to drive expiry; sleeping/offline owners and disconnected peers can retain older content until reconciliation. Lowering retention is not an immediate physical-space guarantee. Candidate selection is bounded, but duplicate/pending checks and causal projection still traverse the retained history journal; full-scale latency remains an acceptance gate.

Expiry uses the same deletion and ciphertext-erasure path as explicit removal. Permanent native-identity proofs prevent late re-import and exact old-envelope replay from restoring expired content. Connected clients remove matching timeline/journal plaintext, and authenticated purge replaces live local/relay ciphertext with original header/digest receipts. V1 retains causal proofs and journal receipts; no journal compaction or secure physical-page erasure is claimed.

Native browser history, independently retained exports, historical backups, filesystem snapshots and old SQLite/WAL pages can retain copies. Backup expiration and older-backup recovery are separate policies. General quarantine/unresolved/shared capture-copy retention and native acceptance remain open.

Ten unit/database tests cover settings/reopening/export, strict original-time boundaries, opt-in behavior, pending and duplicate protection, source ownership, failed-write rollback, bounded restart progress, non-starvation, concurrent workers, late re-import and a clock-rolled-back empty range. A real Rust-process integration proves old pending content uploads before expiry, owner-policy reopening, peer convergence, fresh bootstrap and live SQL ciphertext removal. The settings UI is verified with synthetic replies at desktop and 390 px; this is not native Helium storage or lifecycle evidence.

## Session archive retention backend

The 2026-10-03 backend checkpoint implements an opt-in source-owned policy, initially off, with 30 days, 100 archives and 50 MiB of snapshot content. Accepted ranges are 1–3,650 days, 1–10,000 archives and 1–1,024 MiB. Settings persist with the profile and its replica export; session controls are not connected to the options UI yet.

The combined archive pool includes closed and explicitly saved previous snapshots plus superseded current snapshots. The latest complete current snapshot is excluded. Age uses strict original `captured_at < now − days`; equal/newer captures remain unless count or size requires eviction. Count and byte pressure evict the oldest eligible capture first, then source revision and UUID break ties. Bytes mean UTF-8 serialized snapshot content, not ciphertext, all retained copies or physical disk usage. A source's policy cannot expire another installation's archives.

Every unencrypted draft, missing ACK or queued multipart fragment protects its whole snapshot. Incomplete assemblies are never candidates. The last fully acknowledged current snapshot also stays protected while a newer current replacement uploads. Running and blocked local restoration jobs protect their source archives. These protections can keep the pool above a cap; expiry resumes after acknowledgement or restoration completion/cancellation. A remote owner's expiry cannot remove another device's already active restoration data, which is embedded in that job. New jobs atomically check expiry before saving, preventing a stale selection from starting after expiry commits.

Each pass creates one encrypted session schema-2 expiry operation containing at most 100 target identities. Targets bind the original source revision, kind and fragment count; the expiry author owns all targets and its counter follows their complete fragment ranges. Projection rejects conflicting source/snapshot identities and overlapping counter claims. Proof, counter reservation, projection and journal plaintext cleanup commit atomically; failed writes roll back. Reopening/repeated workers do not duplicate completed expiry, and expired identities stay suppressed even if their original full fragments arrive later. The policy runs after the initial relay pull/epoch check, before uploads, so old pending content uploads first and becomes eligible on a subsequent pass.

Expired saved snapshots disappear from the projection, and stored fragment plaintext becomes metadata-only receipts. Original encrypted envelopes remain immutable. Terminal restoration jobs for expired snapshots are removed on reconciliation; active jobs retain the data needed to finish or cancel safely. Closed-capture fingerprints, live client/relay ciphertext, historical exports/backups, filesystem snapshots and SQLite/WAL pages can still retain copies. Authenticated ciphertext purge and closed-capture cleanup remain required; this checkpoint does not establish a disk-space limit, complete erasure or safe older-backup recovery.

Selection/protection and projection traverse the retained session journal. Only the deletion batch is bounded; the 205-archive restart test is functional evidence, not representative-scale latency. Offline owners/peers may retain archives until reconnect, and several reconciliation passes may be needed to reach limits. Pending expiry proofs preserve their immutable retry identity after a lost reply.

Session fragment schema 1 remains supported. Expiry adds encrypted session payload schema 2 under the unchanged protocol-1 envelope domain, with no relay migration. Earlier extension clients reject these records and stop the affected pull page, preserving their cursor/data until updated. All clients must be updated before retention is enabled; stable extension distribution/update preservation and native acceptance remain gates. No native test profile has received or enabled this backend yet.

Twelve retention tests cover defaults/ranges/persistence/export, strict age/source boundaries, count and UTF-8 size pressure, pending multipart and incomplete protection, active/terminal jobs, write rollback, concurrent workers, capacity-safe proof staging, 100-target batches/reopening, authenticated target identity and replay, lost replies and peer/fresh bootstrap. An additional restore test forces expiry between incarnation lookup and durable job creation. A real Rust-process integration verifies pending uploads before expiry, a discarded committed reply, client/relay restart, owner-policy persistence and fresh bootstrap; it explicitly observes that original SQL ciphertext is still present. Full suite: 250 TypeScript tests and 17 real-process integrations. Session controls, ciphertext/capture-copy cleanup, representative scale, native retention and full backup/recovery policy remain open.
